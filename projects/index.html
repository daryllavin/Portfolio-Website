LiDAR-Based Gait Analysis

As part of my research at Yale, I built a system that uses the iPad's built-in LiDAR sensor for accessible, automated detection of Parkinson's and related movement disorders. I wrote Python tools with OpenCV and Open3D to convert LiDAR recordings into depth maps and 3D reconstructions, for both single frames and full video.

I then integrated Google's MediaPipe pose estimation to track key joints (knees, hips, heels, shoulders, elbows, and wrists), mapped them onto the depth data to get 3D joint trajectories and angles over time, and used polynomial detrending to measure joint angles, stride length, and walking speed. The results showed clear differences between normal walking and Parkinsonian gait.

DreamCatcher

DreamCatcher is a project I built with two friends at CMU's 2025 TartanHacks hackathon. It decodes brainwave (EEG) activity recorded during sleep and turns it into AI-generated videos of dreams that people can watch and share. The EEG decoding is trained through temporal signal masking from the DreamDiffusion model, and CLIP and Stable Diffusion align the EEG signals with text and image embeddings. Users can extend a dream into a video, post it to a shared feed, and get a short AI analysis of it.

We won Best Use of Generative AI and second place in AppLovin's Interactive Content track, the event's largest prize track. We took home $3k+ and were invited to present to AppLovin's CTO and engineering team in Palo Alto.

Trioshield Glove

I was one of sixteen students selected for the 2025 Rethink the Rink Make-A-Thon, a week-long program run by CMU, the Pittsburgh Penguins, Covestro, and Bauer, where my team prototyped a better hockey glove. As a lifelong hockey player, I got a lot out of talking with NHL players about their concerns and learning from materials experts at Covestro and Bauer. We used CAD and Arduino tools to model design changes and measure impact forces on the glove.

Our design reduced palm wear, lowered the glove's weight, and added a safer wrist guard while costing less than the original. Covestro licensed it for $8k at the end of the week.

Bias Detection

A research project from high school on detecting bias in natural language, focused on Generation Z. We wrote a Python program that uses Word2vec to give words bias scores, calibrated with biased word pairs. To get calibration data, we surveyed hundreds of Gen Z respondents across categories like race, gender, and income, then used K-means clustering to find patterns specific to Gen Z, such as how often social justice themes came up. Our findings were published in IEEE Access.
